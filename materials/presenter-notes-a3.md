# Presenter notes · Leader session 3 · The one-truth problem

## Run of show (45 min)

| Minutes | Beat |
|---------|------|
| 0-3 | Welcome. Open cold with the scene: "Monday. Marketing: 214k. Finance: 198k. Forty minutes gone." Then the twist: both are right. |

| 33-41 | The handshake: owner + written definition + test. Then the Daybreak 60-minute meeting, beat by beat - the RENAME move (revenue vs gross demand) is the gift to send them home with. |
| 41-45 | Q&A + homework: run the 15-minute metric audit on their own board pack before a4. |

## Likely exec questions + crisp answers

- "So who was wrong, marketing or finance?" - Neither. Two questions wore one name. The fix is naming both: net revenue AND gross demand.
- "Isn't this the data team's job to prevent?" - They build the machinery (dims, metric layer, tests). Definitions are business calls - delegating them to whoever writes the query is how this started.
- "How much does a metric layer cost?" - Far less than the meetings it deletes. It is mostly discipline plus existing tooling; start with your top ten board metrics, not all of them.
- "We have a data dictionary - same thing?" - Only if it has owners and tests. A wiki nobody enforces is where definitions go to drift.
- "What if two owners disagree?" - Then you have found a real strategy question wearing a metrics costume. Escalate the business question, not the SQL.
