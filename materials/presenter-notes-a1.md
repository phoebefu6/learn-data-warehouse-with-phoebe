# Presenter notes · Leader session 1 · Why a warehouse

## Run of show (45 min)

| Minutes | Beat |
|---------|------|
| 0-3 | Welcome. Frame the track: "no code, ever - this is the boardroom view". Promise: by minute 45 they can define a warehouse in one sentence and know when to refuse one. |
| 3-10 | The frozen-checkout story. Tell it slowly - it is the emotional anchor for the whole track. Land on "two kinds of work, one machine". |
| 10-16 | Two-lane SVG. Walk left lane first (their app), then right (their questions). Ask the room: "which lane do YOUR questions live in?" |
| 16-20 | Why "just add an index" fails. Use the book-index analogy. Deliver the CFO boardroom moment verbatim - it is the quotable of the session. |
| 20-30 | What a warehouse is: extract, restructure, point everything at the copy. Pipeline SVG left to right. Stress "the copy is a feature". |
| 30-34 | Cost shape: storage is parking, compute is fuel, salaries are drivers. Let that metaphor breathe. |
| 34-42 | When NOT to build. The "not yet" profile, then the three buy signals. Poll the room on their signal count - this is the liveliest 8 minutes. |
| 42-45 | Q&A + homework pitch: bring your buy-signal score to a2. |

## Likely exec questions + crisp answers

- "Can't we just buy a faster database?" - Faster app databases take orders faster. Your question reads four years of orders; that wants a different layout, which means a copy.
- "How stale is the copy?" - Minutes to hours, by design. For "how did June go", staleness costs nothing. For "is the site up", use the app - wrong tool otherwise.
- "What does one cost?" - Storage: pennies. Realistic all-in: mostly people. Small team, managed cloud platform: low six figures a year. Session a5 gives the levers.
- "We already have dashboards - don't we have a warehouse?" - Maybe. Ask what they read from. Dashboards on the production database are the frozen-checkout story on a timer.
- "Is AI making this obsolete?" - Opposite. Every AI initiative eats clean, historical, governed data - which is exactly what a warehouse produces.
